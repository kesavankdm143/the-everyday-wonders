Gold is one of the oldest things people have valued. It has been made into jewellery, coins and ornaments for thousands of years, and in India it is part of weddings, festivals and family savings. But what is it about gold that makes people trust it as a store of value?

## What makes gold special

Gold is a chemical element, with the symbol Au. Unlike iron, it does not rust, and it does not easily react with air or water, so a gold ornament can stay bright for centuries. It is soft enough to be shaped by hand, yet it cannot be manufactured: there is only so much of it in the ground, and mining more takes time and effort. Each year's new supply is small compared with all the gold that already exists above ground.

## Why people trust it

Because gold lasts and is hard to produce, it has been accepted as valuable in many places and in many ages. Central banks around the world hold gold as part of their reserves, and many families keep it as jewellery or coins. Unlike a company's shares or a currency, gold is not a promise made by anyone. That is a big part of its appeal when people are worried about the economy.

## 'Holds its value' is not 'never falls'

It is important to be honest about what that phrase means. Gold's price rises and falls, sometimes sharply, and it can stay low for long periods. Gold also does not pay interest or dividends, so it earns nothing while you hold it. 'Holds its value' really means that, over very long periods, people have kept accepting gold as valuable. It does not mean that its price is safe from falling.

## What moves the price

The gold price is influenced by many things, including interest rates, the strength of currencies, and how worried or confident people feel about the future. In India, the price you see also depends on the international gold price, the rupee–dollar exchange rate, and local taxes and duties.

## Key points

- Gold lasts, is hard to produce, and has been valued across many cultures for thousands of years.
- It is not a promise made by any company or government.
- Its price can still fall, and it pays no interest.
- Prices in India depend on the world price, the exchange rate and taxes.
