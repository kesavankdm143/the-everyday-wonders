Bitcoin is the best-known cryptocurrency, a form of digital money that exists only on computers and the internet. Many other cryptocurrencies exist. This guide explains the basic ideas, and why such assets are considered risky.

## What Bitcoin is

Bitcoin was introduced in 2009. Unlike rupees or dollars, it is not issued by any government or bank. Instead, it runs on a network of computers that follow shared rules.

## The ledger

Every Bitcoin transaction is recorded in a public record called a blockchain, which is copied across many computers. Because many copies must agree, it is hard for any one person to change the record.

## Limited supply

Bitcoin's rules limit the total number that can ever be created to 21 million. Some people see this as a strength, but a limited supply does not guarantee that the price will rise.

## Why the price moves so much

Bitcoin's price is set by what people are willing to pay for it. It is not tied to a company's profit or to a physical object, so sentiment, news and regulation can move it sharply. Large rises and falls over short periods are common.

## Risks to understand

- The price can fall sharply and may not recover.
- If you lose the private key or password to your wallet, your holdings may be lost for good, with no one to recover them for you.
- Scams are common in the crypto world, including fake schemes that promise guaranteed returns.
- Rules and tax treatment can change. In India, crypto is subject to specific tax rules and regulatory attention, so check the current position.

## Key points

- Bitcoin is digital money that no government or bank issues.
- Transactions are recorded on a shared public ledger called a blockchain.
- Its price is very volatile and rests on what people will pay.
- Crypto carries risks of loss, lost access and scams.
